### GPR120 signaling

GPR120 belongs to the rhodopsin family of GPCRs, which share a common seven-transmembrane (7TM) domain structure. <u>The 7TM domain is responsible for coupling with different types of G proteins and β-arrestins upon ligand binding. The N-terminal domain of GPR120 contains several extracellular loops that are involved in ligand recognition and binding. The C-terminal domain of GPR120 contains several intracellular loops that are involved in receptor internalization and desensitization.</u>

GPR120 has a typical structure of a class A rhodopsin-like GPCR, consisting of seven transmembrane helices (TM1-TM7), three extracellular loops (ECL1-ECL3), three intracellular loops (ICL1-ICL3), an extracellular N-terminal domain (NTD), and an intracellular C-terminal domain (CTD). Each domain has a specific function in ligand recognition, receptor activation, or signal transduction.

The NTD contains a conserved motif called FFA motif that is essential for binding long-chain fatty acids. The ECLs form a lid-like structure over the binding pocket that modulates ligand access and selectivity. The TM helices form a hydrophobic cavity that accommodates the acyl chain of the fatty acid ligands. The ICLs interact with various intracellular proteins such as G proteins or beta-arrestins that mediate signal transduction from the receptor to downstream effectors. The CTD contains several phosphorylation sites that regulate receptor desensitization and internalization.

The endogenous ligands of GPR120 are medium- and long-chain free fatty acids (FFAs), especially those with unsaturated bonds. Among them, omega-3 fatty acids such as docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) and eicosapentaenoic acid (EPA) have been shown to have potent **anti-inflammatory and insulin-sensitizing** effects via GPR120 activation. Other FFAs such as oleic acid, linoleic acid and palmitoleic acid can also activate GPR120 with varying degrees of efficacy.

The activation of GPR120 by FFAs leads to different downstream signaling pathways depending on the type of ligand and the cellular context. Generally speaking, GPR120 can couple with three major types of G proteins: **Gs, Gi and Gq** [^16 ^]. The coupling with Gs leads to increased intracellular cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) levels and protein kinase A (PKA) activation. The coupling with Gi leads to decreased intracellular cAMP levels and inhibition of adenylyl cyclase. The coupling with Gq leads to increased intracellular calcium levels and protein kinase C (PKC) activation. In addition to these canonical pathways, GPR120 can also recruit **β-arrestins** that mediate receptor internalization, desensitization and alternative signaling cascades such as extracellular signal-regulated kinase (ERK), Akt and AMP-activated protein kinase (AMPK)[ ^4 ^].

### GPR120 response to different saturated/unsaturated fatty acids

GPR120 responds differently to saturated and unsaturated fatty acids, depending on their **chain length and degree of unsaturation**. Saturated fatty acids with 14 or more carbons can activate GPR120 weakly or moderately, while unsaturated fatty acids with 18 or more carbons can activate GPR120 strongly. Among the unsaturated fatty acids, omega-3 fatty acids have higher potency than omega-6 or omega-9 fatty acids. The position and configuration of the double bonds also affect the activation of GPR120. For example, DHA has higher efficacy than EPA at GPR120, and cis-fatty acids have higher affinity than trans-fatty acids at GPR120. For example, DHA (22:6n-3) is more potent than EPA (20:5n-3) or alpha-linolenic acid (ALA; 18:3n-3), and EPA is more potent than arachidonic acid (AA; 20:4n-6) or oleic acid (OA; 18:1n-9) . The differential activation of GPR120 by unsaturated LCFAs leads to biased signaling through different downstream pathways.

The differential response of GPR120 to various fatty acids leads to biased signaling through different downstream pathways. For instance, DHA preferentially activates beta-arrestin-mediated signaling over Gq-mediated signaling at GPR120, while EPA preferentially activates both pathways equally. Beta-arrestin-mediated signaling is associated with anti-inflammatory effects of omega-3 fatty acids, while Gq-mediated signaling is associated with GLP-1 secretion and insulin sensitization.
